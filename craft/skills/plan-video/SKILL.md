# Plan a video

Package first: the title and thumbnail idea come before the beats, because every beat pays off what the package promised. Read `brief.md`, `studio/research/videos.json` (the notes, the outliers) and the user's own notes.

## studio/plan.md, in this order

1. **The premise and the promise** (2 to 3 lines): the premise in one sentence a stranger can picture (a test with an uncertain ending, no insider words), then what the viewer gets or sees happen. Specific: a number, a result, a stake. Say the viewer's own pain or wish in their words. If the video shows what a tool does, the premise is the general thing it does for anyone; the one case you play through is the proof, not the topic.
2. **Package ideas**: 3 working titles from the research's winning shapes, and one thumbnail idea for each (one picture, 0 to 4 words). They are drafts; the Titles stage writes 30 to 50 premise lines and judges them, and only the ones that pass get thumbnails. A thumbnail idea must be clear to a stranger at a glance (the topic in plain words, three elements at most).
3. **The 3 questions** the package raises in a viewer's head. The video answers all of them.
4. **The hook (first 30 s)**: start in the most tense or surprising moment; the first 5 seconds confirm the click (show the thing the thumbnail shows). No greeting, no "in this video".
5. **The beats**: a numbered list. For each: what is said (one line), what is on screen (camera, screen capture, a result), and the turn ("but", "so") into the next beat. A re-hook about every minute; a payoff before a third of the runtime.
6. **The ending**: the biggest reveal or result last, then one line into a next video. Never a slow wind-down.
7. **What to film**: a checklist the user takes to the recording: shots, screens to capture, any photo for the thumbnail (a few expressions, plain background).
8. **Length**: a target in minutes, from the beats.

## Rules

- Only promise what the user can really show. If the video would not deliver the thumbnail's promise in some form on screen, change the promise.
- Write in the user's voice and language from the brief; short lines.
- If `studio/plan.md` exists, the user may have edited it in the Studio: keep their words and change only what the stage or their notes ask.
- A script, if the brief asks for one, follows `script-video` into `studio/script.md`.

The plan-review gate shows the plan in the Studio's Plan page; the user approves it or sends it back with notes.
